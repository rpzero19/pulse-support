Pulse Privacy Policy

Last updated 1 November 2026

This policy covers Pulse for iPhone and Pulse for Windows.

The short version

Pulse does not collect, transmit, sell or share any of your data. Your heart rate goes from your strap to your iPhone or Windows PC over Bluetooth and stays there.

What Pulse reads

Pulse connects over Bluetooth to a heart rate strap you choose (for example a WHOOP strap with Heart Rate Broadcast turned on) and reads the live heart rate it broadcasts. Bluetooth is used only for this.

What Pulse stores

Only on your device:

  • your most recent reading and its time (iPhone, for the Home Screen widget);
  • one-minute heart rate averages from the last 3 days, used to estimate your resting heart rate for the stress estimate;
  • stress readings you enter yourself to calibrate the stress estimate;
  • an identifier for your strap, so Pulse can reconnect to it;
  • your settings (and on Windows, where you placed the widget).

The last few minutes of readings are kept in memory for the trend line and the stress estimate. On Windows, Pulse may also write a small error log on your PC to help diagnose connection problems; it contains no heart rate data.

What Pulse does not do

There is no account and no sign-in. Pulse has no servers, makes no network requests, contains no analytics, advertising or third-party SDKs, and does not track you across apps or websites. It does not read or write Apple Health data.

Lock Screen, Dynamic Island, widgets and the Windows tray

On iPhone, the Lock Screen, Dynamic Island and widget are drawn on your device by iOS from the data above. On Windows, the floating widget and the number in the notification area are drawn on your PC. Nothing is sent through a server, and Pulse does not use push notifications.

Backups

If you back up your iPhone (iCloud Backup or a computer) or your PC, Pulse's small amount of stored data may be included in that backup under the terms of the backup service. Pulse itself never receives it.

Deleting your data

Deleting the app removes everything Pulse has stored. On iPhone, "Reset calibration" in the app also deletes your calibration readings. On Windows, uninstalling Pulse removes its data; the portable version keeps it in the Pulse folder inside your AppData folder, which you can delete.

Children

Pulse collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Not a medical device

Pulse shows the readings your strap provides, and its stress figure is an estimate worked out from your heart rate. It is not a medical device and is not meant to diagnose, treat or prevent any condition.
